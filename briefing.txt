🏢 3. TECH COMPANY DEMAND - POTENTIAL BUYERS

• Firmus Technologies Commits US$300 Million to SUBCO's APX East Trans-Pacific
  Submarine Cable as Anchor Customer, Directly Connecting SA AI Factory Sites
  to US Hyperscale Cloud Buyers — Confirms Real Hyperscale Demand for South
  Australia-Based AI Infrastructure is Now Contracting at Capacity Scale
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]: Australian AI infrastructure company Firmus Technologies
  has entered a landmark 25-year, US$300 million (approximately A$418 million)
  agreement with submarine cable developer SUBCO, securing up to 150 Terabits
  per second of dedicated international bandwidth as anchor customer for SUBCO's
  planned APX East (Asia Pacific Express East) trans-Pacific subsea cable system;
  the cable is designed as Australia's first sovereign-owned express international
  hypercable and targets commercial activation in Q4 2028, purpose-built to
  connect Firmus's nationwide AI Factory compute clusters — including its
  renewable-powered sites in South Australia at Tailem Bend (up to 1.0 GW IT
  load capacity) and Stirling North near Port Augusta (up to 1.3 GW IT load
  capacity) — directly to US hyperscalers and AI-native developers; the deal
  confirms that real hyperscale demand is already contracting connectivity
  capacity in anticipation of SA AI factory sites coming online from 2028
  onwards, and that the SA sites are being positioned not merely as domestic
  compute nodes but as anchor points in an international AI token production
  and export corridor spanning the trans-Pacific; for South Energy, the
  Firmus-SUBCO agreement is among the clearest buyer-signal events yet
  published for the SA data centre market — a US$300 million, 25-year
  infrastructure commitment specifically to serve the same geographic corridor
  (Tailem Bend, Port Augusta/Stirling North) where South Energy holds or
  targets land positions, confirming that international hyperscale buyers
  are already purchasing access to those sites at a capacity and tenure scale
  that validates long-term exit valuations; any SA project with existing or
  adjacent land to Firmus's SA corridors now benefits from demonstrated
  international connectivity infrastructure, not just projected connectivity,
  strengthening the site's completeness assessment against the full buyer
  checklist: land, grid, renewable supply, planning, and now confirmed
  international data transit.
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• PM Albanese Concedes to Queensland and NT at National Cabinet — Australian
  Government Effectively Drops Mandatory National BYO Renewables Rule for Data
  Centres, Fragmenting the Compliance Framework and Increasing Relative Value
  of SA Projects Already Contracted with Renewables [HIGH RELEVANCE]: Multiple
  credible sources including Data Center Dynamics, ESD News, and RenewEconomy
  report that Prime Minister Anthony Albanese conceded to Queensland Premier
  David Crisafulli and the Northern Territory at the National Cabinet meeting,
  with Queensland and the Northern Territory winning the ability to use fossil
  fuels — coal and gas — to power data centres rather than being bound by a
  national BYO renewables mandate; Data Center Dynamics reports the Australian
  government "dropped plans for a data center renewable energy mandate" following
  objections from both jurisdictions; ESD News reports "Qld and NT win fight to
  use fossil fuels to power data centres"; this represents a material shift from
  PM Albanese's July 15 announcement that data centres would face a "legal
  obligation" to be net generators of renewable energy — the National Cabinet
  outcome substitutes a case-by-case exemption pathway for a universal binding
  standard, meaning the national framework is now split between states that
  voluntarily apply the BYO renewables standard and states (QLD/NT) that can
  apply to bypass it through the Australian Energy Regulator; for South Energy,
  this is a paradoxical positive: South Australia's state government has
  independently committed to a fully renewable grid and introduced dedicated
  data centre and AI infrastructure legislation centred on renewable-powered
  development — which means SA projects already carry binding renewable supply
  at the state level and are definitively compliant with any version of the
  national standard without requiring exemption applications, appeals, or AER
  review; this eliminates the most significant approval risk that buyers face
  when evaluating QLD or NT alternatives, and means SA's regulatory compliance
  position is now more predictable and buyer-ready than any other jurisdiction
  in Australia that depends on the federal process for mandate compliance.
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]

• Energy Minister Bowen Insists Federal Legislation Will Still Mandate 100%
  Renewables Plus Firming Nationally — But Introduces AER Case-by-Case
  Exemption Pathway That Creates Compliance Uncertainty for All Non-SA
  Jurisdictions [HIGH RELEVANCE]: In direct response to reports that the
  national BYO renewables mandate had been abandoned, Climate Change and Energy
  Minister Chris Bowen stated that the federal government "will legislate binding
  national standards for data centres across the country without exception, which
  will require 100% renewables backed by firming, which can be gas," but
  clarified that the rules will allow state-owned fossil fuel assets to make a
  case-by-case argument to the Australian Energy Regulator (AER) to power data
  centres with fossil fuels, with the Commonwealth — not the state — making the
  final exemption decision; this Bowen clarification, covered by both RenewEconomy
  ("'No carve-outs' for states over renewables mandate for data centres, Bowen
  says") and ESD News ("Bowen insists no 'carve-outs' in data centre
  legislation"), introduces a significant new element that further differentiates
  SA from every other jurisdiction: whether Bowen's interpretation or the QLD/NT
  position prevails legislatively, buyers seeking certainty on compliance must
  now either accept a multi-year AER exemption review process or locate in a
  jurisdiction where the renewable mandate is met without application; South
  Australia is the only state in Australia where both the state government
  independently mandates renewable-powered development and where the transmission
  grid already achieves over 74% renewable generation, meaning the federal
  mandate compliance question is resolved at the state infrastructure layer
  before it reaches the federal legislative layer; for South Energy, the
  Bowen-vs-states policy tension is a direct commercial accelerant — buyers
  with tight deployment timelines and ESG mandates (hyperscalers, institutional
  data centre platforms, global PE-backed developers) cannot afford to wait for
  an AER exemption ruling that may take 12 to 24 months, and will prioritise
  jurisdictions with pre-resolved compliance; every week of federal policy
  uncertainty adds urgency to SA project acquisitions as the only available
  exit from the uncertainty.

🏢 3. 科技公司需求（潛在買家）

• Firmus科技以3億美元主力客戶身份加入SUBCO APX East跨太平洋海底光纜，
  直接連通南澳州AI工廠選址與美國超大規模雲端買家——確認對南澳州AI基礎
  設施的超大規模真實需求已在容量規模層面完成合約落實【高度相關】：澳大
  利亞AI基礎設施企業Firmus科技與海底光纜開發商SUBCO達成一項為期25年、
  金額高達3億美元（約4.18億澳元）的里程碑協議，以主力錨定客戶身份獲得
  SUBCO計劃建設的APX East（亞太快速東線）跨太平洋海底光纜系統150太比特
  每秒（Tbps）專屬國際帶寬；該光纜定位為澳大利亞首條主權所有的快速國際
  超級光纜，目標2028年第四季度商業投運，專門連接Firmus旗下全國各地AI工
  廠計算集群——包括其在南澳州塔萊姆本德（Tailem Bend，IT負荷容量最高達
  1.0吉瓦）和奧古斯塔港附近斯特靈北（Stirling North，IT負荷容量最高達
  1.3吉瓦）的可再生能源驅動場址——直接連通美國超大規模雲端客戶及AI原生
  開發商；這筆交易確認，真實的超大規模需求已在南澳州AI工廠場址2028年後
  陸續投運之前，在連通容量層面完成合約布局，南澳州場址被定位為橫跨太平
  洋的國際AI算力生產與出口走廊上的錨定節點，而非單純的國內計算節點；對
  South Energy而言，Firmus與SUBCO的協議是迄今南澳州數據中心市場已發布
  的買方信號事件中最為清晰的之一——一筆專門服務於South Energy持有或目標
  的土地位置所在地理走廊（塔萊姆本德、奧古斯塔港/斯特靈北）、金額3億美
  元、期限25年的基礎設施承諾，確認國際超大規模買方已以足以支撐長期退出
  估值的容量規模和協議期限購入這些場址的接入資源；任何毗鄰或位於Firmus
  南澳州走廊的場址，均因此獲得已落地的國際連通基礎設施背書——而非僅有
  預測性連通——進一步強化場址針對買方完整性評估清單各項的達標程度：土
  地、電網、可再生能源供應、規劃審批，以及現在確認落實的國際數據傳輸。

📜 4. 政策與法規（南澳州 + 全國）

• 澳大利亞總理阿爾巴尼斯在全國內閣會議上向昆士蘭州和北領地妥協——聯邦
  政府實質上放棄對數據中心強制推行的全國「自帶可再生能源」規定，合規框
  架碎片化，令已簽訂可再生能源合約的南澳州項目相對價值大幅提升【高度相
  關】：Data Center Dynamics、ESD News及RenewEconomy多家權威媒體報道，
  總理阿爾巴尼斯在全國內閣會議上向昆士蘭州長克里薩福利（Crisafulli）
  及北領地妥協，昆士蘭州和北領地獲准使用化石燃料（煤炭和天然氣）為數
  據中心供電，而非受制於全國性「自帶可再生能源」強制規定；Data Center
  Dynamics報道，澳大利亞政府「取消了對數據中心可再生能源強制規定的計
  劃」；ESD News報道「昆士蘭州和北領地贏得使用化石燃料為數據中心供電
  的鬥爭」；這是對總理阿爾巴尼斯7月15日公告——數據中心將面臨「法律義
  務」成為可再生能源淨發電主體——的重大背離；全國內閣結果以逐案豁免路
  徑取代了全國統一約束性標準，意味著全國框架已一分為二：自願適用「自帶
  可再生能源」標準的州份，以及可申請豁免的州份（昆士蘭州/北領地）；對
  South Energy而言，這在矛盾中創造了利好：南澳州州政府已獨立作出全面
  可再生電網承諾，並推出以可再生能源供電為核心的專項數據中心及AI基礎設
  施立法——這意味着南澳州項目已在州層面實現具有約束力的可再生能源供應，
  无需豁免申請、上訴或澳大利亞能源監管機構（AER）審查即可明確符合任何
  版本的全國標準；這消除了買方評估昆士蘭州或北領地替代方案時面臨的最大
  審批風險，使南澳州的監管合規立場比任何依賴聯邦程序達成強制合規的澳大
  利亞司法管轄區都更具可預期性和買方就緒性。【高度相關】

• 能源部長鮑文堅持聯邦立法仍將全國性強制100%可再生能源加調峰——但引入
  澳大利亞能源監管機構（AER）逐案豁免路徑，為所有非南澳州司法管轄區製
  造合規不確定性【高度相關】：針對全國「自帶可再生能源」強制規定已遭放
  棄的報道，氣候變化與能源部長克里斯·鮑文（Chris Bowen）聲明，聯邦政府
  「將立法在全國範圍對數據中心推行具有約束力的標準，毫無例外，要求100%
  可再生能源配合調峰（調峰可由天然氣承擔）」；但鮑文同時澄清，相關規定
  將允許州屬化石燃料資產向澳大利亞能源監管機構（AER）逐案申請以化石燃
  料（天然氣或煤炭）為數據中心供電，且最終豁免決定權歸聯邦而非申請州政
  府；鮑文的澄清聲明分別被RenewEconomy（「鮑文：各州不得就可再生能源強
  制規定獲得'例外豁免'」）和ESD News（「鮑文堅稱數據中心立法無例外豁
  免」）報道，引入了一個進一步凸顯南澳州差異化優勢的重要新元素：無論鮑
  文的立法解讀最終勝出還是昆士蘭州/北領地的立場主導，尋求合規確定性的
  買方現在必須要麼接受可能歷時12至24個月的AER豁免審查程序，要麼選擇在
  無需申請即可達標的司法管轄區落戶；南澳州是澳大利亞唯一一個州政府獨立
  強制推行可再生能源供電開發、且輸電電網本身已實現逾74%可再生能源發電
  比例的州份，意味着聯邦強制合規問題在聯邦立法層面到達之前已在州基礎設
  施層面得到解決；對South Energy而言，鮑文與各州之間的政策張力是直接的
  商業催化劑——持有緊迫部署時間表和ESG授權的買方（超大規模雲端客戶、機
  構數據中心平台、全球私募股權支持的開發商）無法承受等候可能耗時1至2年
  的AER豁免裁決，必然優先考慮合規問題已預先解決的司法管轄區；聯邦政策
  每持續一周的不確定性，都在為南澳州項目收購增加緊迫性——那是擺脫不確
