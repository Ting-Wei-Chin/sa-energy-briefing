📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• National Cabinet QLD/NT Carve-Out: Coal and Gas Permitted for Data Centres in Two States —
  SA Structural Compliance Advantage Confirmed [HIGH RELEVANCE]:
  Analysis reveals that Queensland and the Northern Territory declined to adopt the renewable
  energy tie-in component of National Cabinet's 26 August 2026 mandatory data centre standards,
  permitting data centre operators in those states to draw from coal and gas generation. This
  creates a two-tier compliance landscape nationally: SA, with its 100% net renewable target
  and structurally green grid, satisfies the federal energy standards by default, while QLD/NT
  operators will require additional offsetting arrangements if national legislation proceeds.
  Sources: The Conversation | https://theconversation.com/australias-backdown-on-data-centres-allows-them-to-use-energy-from-coal-and-gas-heres-what-that-means-290613
  Clayton Utz | https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/august/nine-governments-one-rulebook-national-cabinet-backs-mandatory-ai-and-data-centre-standards
  [HIGH RELEVANCE — the QLD/NT carve-out bifurcates the Australian data centre market into
  high-compliance (SA, VIC) and structurally at-risk (QLD, NT) zones; for hyperscalers with
  ESG mandates or anticipating post-2027 federal legislation, SA site selection becomes
  structurally preferred over QLD; South Energy's site marketing can now quantify the
  compliance premium of SA over QLD — directly actionable for buyer due diligence and
  REGO compliance comparisons]

  [MONITORING] ECMC September 2026 regulatory package (King & Wood Mallesons, 14 September
  analysis): (1) mandatory REGO-backed renewable energy and firming offset obligation for
  data centres; (2) AEMC full capacity charging cost recovery rule; (3) AEMC 30 MW+
  technical grid standards. Only sites with pre-secured firm transmission in renewable-rich
  locations satisfy all three obligations at connection without additional capital investment.

  [MONITORING] Australia to legislate mandatory net-generator requirement for large data
  centres — operators must inject at least as much power as they consume and underwrite new
  firmed renewable supply. Federal draft legislation targeting early 2027 Parliament
  introduction.

  [MONITORING] Non-retrospective intent for federal mandatory standards confirmed at National
  Cabinet (26 August 2026). Projects obtaining SA Coordinator-General approval in 2026 may
  lock in pre-legislation status.

  [MONITORING] SA Government's proposed Data Centre and AI Infrastructure Act (announced June
  2026): data centres to be designated essential infrastructure; Coordinator-General as single
  decision-making authority; "new energy for new demand" requirement; currently in
  consultation and drafting stage.

  [MONITORING] AEMC actively reviewing National Electricity Rule changes for 30 MW+ data
  centres (fault ride-through/LVRT requirement); federal legislation targeted for early 2027.
