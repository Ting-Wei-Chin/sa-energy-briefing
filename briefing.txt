🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• AEMC Proposes Mandatory Grid Connection Standards for Large Data Centres (≥30MW);
  ECMC Agreed in July 2026 to Mandate Renewable Offsets; NER Changes Due for
  Consideration by ECMC in September 2026 [HIGH RELEVANCE]: The Australian Energy
  Market Commission has proposed new technical standards requiring large data centres
  with loads of 30MW or more to remain connected during grid faults rather than
  tripping off — a shift from voluntary best-practice to mandatory performance
  obligation — while the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council (ECMC) agreed
  at its July 2026 meeting to progress regulatory arrangements mandating that data
  centres offset their electricity demand by investing in additional renewable and
  firming generation; National Electricity Rule change requests are expected to be
  considered by ECMC at its September 2026 session, with federal legislation targeted
  for introduction to Parliament in early 2027, and battery storage is emerging as
  the primary compliance pathway for operators required to meet renewable offset
  obligations; for South Energy, the emerging mandatory compliance framework creates
  a structural differentiator for SA project sites — SA's existing high renewable
  penetration and its trajectory toward 100% net renewable electricity by end-2027
  means SA-based data centre projects will satisfy the federal renewable offset
  mandate with materially lower additional investment than competitors located in
  coal-heavy states, and projects in the Bundey and Port Augusta corridors with
  contracted renewable supply or co-located solar/BESS can present a documented,
  AEMC-aligned compliance pathway to hyperscaler buyers who are already preparing
  legal and operational frameworks for mandatory net-generator obligations.
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]

  at Bundey; 75+ Prospective Projects from 41 Proponents Now in SA Grid Connection
  Discussions [HIGH RELEVANCE]: ElectraNet's 2026 Transmission Annual Planning Report
  (TAPR) confirms that over 75 prospective connection projects across 41 proponents
  are currently in active discussions to connect to South Australia's transmission
  network — the deepest queue on record — and identifies three priority transmission
  investments: (1) the $3.5 billion Northern Transmission Project, currently in early
  planning, to increase transfer capacity between the Bundey substation (SA's new 330kV

  275/132kV Yadnarie North substation and upgrading the Cultana-Yadnarie lines from
  132kV to 275kV; and (3) the South East Expansion; SA electricity demand is forecast
  to double by 2040, driven by data centres, green steel, hydrogen and mining, with
  the TAPR describing this as a "once-in-a-generation" growth opportunity for the SA
  network; for South Energy, the TAPR directly validates the Bundey corridor as the
  nexus of SA's committed grid investment — the Northern Transmission Project's
  priority was determined by the co-location of confirmed data centre loads at Bundey,
  and acquiring land within or adjacent to this corridor positions projects as
  primary beneficiaries of the state's largest planned transmission expansion, removing
  the transmission capacity risk that constrains competing locations elsewhere in the
  NEM.

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• NSW Releases Australia's First State-Level Data Centre Policy Framework (17 August
  2026); NSW Energy Minister Gains REZ-Style Grid Access Powers Over Data Centres —
  SA's Open-Access Approach Now a Structural Competitive Advantage [HIGH RELEVANCE]:
  On 17 August 2026 the NSW Government released the NSW Data Centre Policy Framework
  and accompanying Guidelines — the first state-level data centre framework in
  Australia — while simultaneously introducing the Electricity Infrastructure
  Investment Amendment Bill 2026 to the NSW Legislative Assembly on 5 August, granting
  the NSW Energy Minister Renewable Energy Zone-style powers to restrict or condition
  grid access for any large-load infrastructure capable of transmitting 5MW or more,
  and enabling the state to require data centre operators to bear the full cost of new
  network infrastructure investment; public submissions on the Bill close 14 September
  2026, with federal legislation targeted for early 2027; for South Energy, NSW's
  framework tightens the regulatory environment for data centre investment in
  Australia's largest existing market at precisely the moment SA is moving in the
  opposite direction — Premier Malinauskas's proposed Data Centre and AI Infrastructure
  Act offers streamlined approvals, the essential infrastructure planning pathway,
  and Coordinator-General decision-making, while NSW introduces ministerial veto
  powers, network cost levies, and mandatory compliance submissions; hyperscalers
  evaluating national site selection after August 2026 face a materially harder,
  slower approvals process in NSW and a demonstrably faster, government-supported
  pathway in SA; South Energy should incorporate this regulatory contrast into every
  SA project information memorandum and buyer presentation as a dated, verifiable
  competitive advantage.
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]

• South Australia's Proposed Data Centre and AI Infrastructure Act: Streamlined
  Approvals, Essential Infrastructure Pathway, Coordinator-General Powers, and
  "New Energy for New Demand" Obligation [HIGH RELEVANCE]: Premier Peter Malinauskas
  announced on 23 June 2026 the SA Data Centre Strategy and the proposed Data Centre
  and AI Infrastructure Act, which would designate data centres as essential
  infrastructure under SA's Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act — enabling
  the Coordinator-General to take on decision-making functions for major projects and
  streamlining the approvals pathway that currently requires multiple agency
  processes; key obligations under the proposed Act include: data centres must
  match demand growth with new firmed supply ("new energy for new demand"), provide
  operational flexibility to support grid stability, comply with SA Technical
  Regulator certification standards, and supply an SA Water certificate confirming
  sufficient water supply; planning regulations were already amended in late 2025 to
  permit data centres through the essential infrastructure pathway; for South Energy,
  the proposed Act validates the SA Government's role as an active enabler — not
  merely a passive regulator — of data centre investment, and the "new energy for
  new demand" obligation, while creating a compliance requirement, is significantly
  easier for SA projects to satisfy than for NSW or Victoria projects, given SA's
  $32.5 billion renewable pipeline and the renewable power agreements already
  accessible in the Bundey and Port Augusta corridors.

  間保持持續接入狀態，而非自動跳閘脫網——此舉標誌著從行業自願最佳實踐向法定強
  制性義務的根本性轉變；與此同時，能源與氣候變化部長理事會（ECMC）在2026年7月
