🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• AEMO ESOO 2026: Australian Data Centre Electricity Demand to Surge 7× by 2035-36
  — SA Grid Connection Scarcity Will Command a Premium: AEMO's 2026 Electricity
  Statement of Opportunities (ESOO), released 25 August 2026, projects that Australian
  data centres will consume 34 terawatt-hours per year by 2035-36, a near-sevenfold
  increase from approximately 5 TWh today — lifting data centres' share of National
  Electricity Market demand from 3% to 13%; the number of data centre projects in active
  development in Australia has more than doubled from 97 to 225 in one year alone.
  For South Energy, the ESOO crystallises the scale of the grid infrastructure race:
  SA sites that can demonstrate pre-secured 330kV transmission capacity at Bundey —
  the only substation in Australia with available high-voltage exit capacity for a
  project of this scale — will command a structural scarcity premium as developers
  nationwide compete for finite connection slots ahead of the forecast demand surge.

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• BREAKING — National Cabinet (27-28 August 2026): PM Albanese Backs Down from Mandatory
  100% Renewables Mandate for Data Centres — "Flexible" Multi-State Framework to Proceed,
  Federal Legislation Deferred to 2027: After Queensland and the Northern Territory
  opposed the federal government's proposed national standard requiring all new large-scale
  data centres to be powered exclusively by renewable energy, Prime Minister Albanese
  conceded at the National Cabinet meeting (27-28 August 2026) and agreed to a more
  "flexible" approach; the Energy and Climate Ministers Council (ECMC) had already agreed
  — with QLD and NT dissenting — in July 2026 to progress mandatory renewables requirements
  through National Electricity Rule changes, to be formally considered at the ECMC's
  September 2026 meeting; new federal legislation is now expected in early 2027. Energy
  Minister Chris Bowen confirmed "data centres are welcome if they bring their own
  additional renewable energy" remains the guiding principle, but the compliance mechanism
  will now be co-developed with states. For South Energy, the policy shift has a directly
  positive commercial implication: SA sites drawing from the state's 100% net renewable
  grid already comply with the strictest version of what the federal framework may yet
  impose — while QLD and NT sites avoid the constraint entirely, making SA's renewable
  credential a voluntary premium rather than a minimum standard. South Energy should
  explicitly position its SA portfolio as "ESG-compliant under any foreseeable version of
  the national framework" in all buyer engagement, targeting US and EU operators who face
  mandatory sustainability reporting obligations that the federal backdown does not relieve.

• AEMC Proposes Mandatory Grid Ride-Through Standards for All Data Centres ≥30MW
  — Final Rule Expected Mid-2026: The Australian Energy Market Commission published a
  draft rule change in March/April 2026 requiring data centres with loads of 30MW or
  more to remain connected to the grid during voltage and frequency disturbances — rather
  than disconnecting and triggering cascading grid failures (as has occurred in Texas and
  Ireland); standards would align with international precedents; stakeholder feedback
  closed 7 May 2026 and the final rule is expected imminently; compliance will be a
  mandatory connection approval condition for any new large load connecting to the NEM,
  and will directly affect the engineering specifications and backup power system design
  for all South Energy project sites. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
