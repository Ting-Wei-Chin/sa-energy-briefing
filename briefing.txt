🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• [UPDATED COUNTDOWN — 13 DAYS] AEMC Final Determination on Data Centre Grid Standards:
  29 October 2026 [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Thirteen days remain to the AEMC's binding technical standard for 30 MW+ data centres
  (disturbance ride-through, system strength self-remediation or charge, alignment with
  Texas/Ireland/Finland benchmarks). Pre-secured firm transmission agreements at Bundey and
  the NTx corridor are specifically structured to satisfy these standards from day one of the
  determination's commencement. Projects still in the connection queue face the highest
  compliance exposure post-determination.
  Sources: AEMC | https://www.aemc.gov.au/rule-changes/improving-nem-access-standards-package-2
  WattClarity | https://wattclarity.com.au/articles/2026/04/05april-aemc-draft-datacentre/

• [NEW] AER Formally Revokes SA Retailer Reliability Obligation for December 2026 –
  February 2027 Summer Period [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  The AER's official reliability obligation register now shows the South Australia December
  2026 – February 2027 instrument as formally revoked following AEMO's October 2025
  notification that no reliability gap is forecast. This formal regulatory revocation —
  distinct from the physical supply solution (AGL's Torrens Island B extension reported
  yesterday) — confirms the SA grid carries zero emergency intervention risk through the
  critical summer energisation window when NTx corridor data centre connections are targeted
  to ramp up. Large industrial loads connecting this summer face no involuntary curtailment
  exposure under the Reliability and Emergency Reserve Trader (RERT) mechanism.
  Source: AER | https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/retail/reliability-obligation/south-australia-december-2026-february-2027

• [NEW] ElectraNet Publishes "Network Connection Opportunities to 2030" (July 2026)
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  ElectraNet's July 2026 document maps remaining available connection capacity at each SA
  substation through to 2030 — providing project developers and land acquirers with
  node-level visibility of where firm connection capacity currently exists and on what
  timeline. More granular than the TAPR's aggregate pipeline overview, this document
  directly supports NTx corridor site selection decisions by identifying which substations
  retain headroom for data centre loads before exhaustion, and which require network
  augmentation. South Energy's existing NTx corridor positions align with substations
  identified as having near-term connection capacity.
  Source: ElectraNet | https://electranet.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Network-Connection-Opportunities-to-2030-FY25-26.pdf

• [NEW] SA Planning Regulation: Data Centre Early Works Now Exempt from Development Approval
  [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Amendments to South Australia's planning regulations (effective 13 August 2026) exempt
  excavation and fill, access road construction, and site fencing from development approval
  requirements at proposed data centre sites — unless the site is in a flood zone or coastal
  area. Published on the SA Planning and Land Use Services portal (plan.sa.gov.au), this early
  works exemption enables physical site preparation on NTx corridor land positions to commence
  before full Coordinator-General project approval is granted. This reduces time-to-market
  versus competing jurisdictions — including NSW (August 2026 guidelines) and Victoria (September
  2026 Action Plan) — where equivalent site works require prior consent. South Energy's NTx
  corridor land positions should be assessed against these exemption criteria to identify which
  sites qualify for immediate early works commencement.
  Source: SA Planning Portal | https://plan.sa.gov.au/news/article/2026/regulation-changes-to-provide-for-data-centre-early-works
