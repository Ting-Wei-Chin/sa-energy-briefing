• BREAKING — Anthropic Signs First Australian Data Centre Deal at
  Queensland's Western Downs Digital Park [HIGH RELEVANCE]:
  Queensland Premier David Crisafulli announced in State Parliament on
  16 September 2026 that AI company Anthropic has executed a lease agreement
  with Zerra DC (Singapore-based AGP Sustainable Real Assets) for the first
  hall of the $32 billion Western Downs Digital Park near Dalby, approximately
  250 km west of Brisbane; the 725-hectare site targets a four-stage 2.16 GW
  build-out, with Anthropic's hall to operate from 2027 on a coal, gas, and
  new-renewables power mix; the project has no pre-secured transmission

  choice of an unverified-grid Queensland site over previously flagged SA

  currently precedes grid certainty at the first-mover decision stage — SA's
  durable counter-argument is total-cost-of-connection compliance (REGO offset
  mandate, AEMC full-capacity charging rule, 30 MW+ technical standards) and
  regulatory risk exposure over the 2027–2030 period versus Queensland's
  coal-mix grid.

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• SA Government's Proposed Data Centre and AI Infrastructure Act — Dedicated
  Legislation Framework Confirmed [HIGH RELEVANCE]:
  Premier Peter Malinauskas announced in June 2026 a new SA Data Centre
  Strategy anchored by a proposed Data Centre and AI Infrastructure Act that
  would designate data centres as essential infrastructure, empower the
  Coordinator-General as single decision-making authority for major projects,
  and require developers to match demand growth with new firmed renewable
  supply ("new energy for new demand"); the Act will mandate operational
  flexibility for grid stability, transparent energy-use reporting, clear
  technical standards, and fair network cost allocation; the legislation is
  currently in consultation and drafting stage with detailed exposure draft
  not yet published. This framework, when enacted, will further entrench SA's
  single-pathway planning advantage over NSW's multi-gateway model and create
  statutory certainty for hyperscale buyer due diligence.
  Source: W.Media | Capital Brief | ABC News (abc.net.au/news/2026-06-23)
  [HIGH RELEVANCE — directly affects planning pathway value for SA projects]

  [MONITORING] ECMC September 2026 regulatory package now formally issued
  (confirmed via King & Wood Mallesons 14 September analysis): (1) mandatory
  REGO-backed renewable energy and firming offset obligation for data centres;
  (2) AEMC "full capacity charging" cost recovery rule; (3) AEMC 30 MW+
  technical grid standards. Only sites with pre-secured firm transmission in
  renewable-rich locations — such as Bundey 330 kV — satisfy all three
  obligations at connection without additional capital investment.

  [MONITORING] Federal mandatory data centre energy and water standards:
  non-retrospective intent confirmed at National Cabinet (26 August 2026).
  Projects obtaining SA Coordinator General approval in 2026 may lock in
  pre-legislation status — directly increasing pipeline value for buyers
  seeking to avoid post-2027 mandatory standards. Federal draft legislation
  targeting early 2027.

  [MONITORING] NSW Electricity Infrastructure Investment Amendment Bill 2026
  consultation closed 14 September 2026. Industry submission volume now under
  analysis; political durability of NSW ministerial gatekeeping unclear.
  SA single-pathway Coordinator General fast-track remains the primary SA
  buyer-conversion argument against NSW.

  [MONITORING] AEMC proposed "full capacity charging" rule and AEMC 30 MW+
  technical standards — both now formally part of the September ECMC package.
  Implication: pre-secured, contracted connections command lower cost exposure
  vs speculative applications.
