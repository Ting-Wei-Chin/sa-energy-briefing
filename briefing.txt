🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• AEMC Final Determination on Data Centre Grid Standards Due Late October 2026 [HIGH RELEVANCE]:
  The Australian Energy Market Commission's draft rule setting new technical grid connection standards
  for large data centres (30 MW+ threshold, disturbance ride-through requirements, international
  alignment with Texas, Ireland and Finland) is now approaching its final determination, due in late
  October 2026. Industry commentary warns of "data centres becoming grid risk assets" as the
  standards harden from draft to binding rule. The final rule will govern all future connection
  agreements nationally and set the technical compliance baseline for SA Coordinator-General-approved
  sites.
  Sources: AEMC | https://www.aemc.gov.au/news-centre/media-releases/aemc-proposes-new-grid-standards-data-centre-connections
  w.media | https://w.media/aemc-warns-on-data-centre-grid-risks-as-new-standards-loom/
  PV Tech | https://www.pv-tech.org/australias-aemc-proposes-new-standards-to-prevent-data-centre-blackouts-amid-rising-energy-demand/
  GridServe | https://gridserve.com.au/data-centres-are-becoming-grid-risk-assets-what-the-new-aemc-draft-rules-mean/
  [HIGH RELEVANCE — Pre-secured firm transmission agreements at Bundey and the NTx corridor are
  specifically structured to satisfy these technical standards. Projects holding existing connection
  agreements face minimal exposure to new compliance costs; projects still in the queue face the
  highest risk. The late-October final determination creates a clear urgency window for connection
  agreement execution before the rule becomes binding.]
