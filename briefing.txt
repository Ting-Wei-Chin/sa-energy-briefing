🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• AEMO Opens Non-Network Options Consultation for Three Newly Actionable 2026 ISP
  Projects — Submissions Close 17 September 2026, Creating Immediate Window for
  Storage and Demand-Response Proponents to Register Alternatives to New
  Transmission Lines [HIGH RELEVANCE]: The Australian Energy Market Operator
  (AEMO) has opened formal consultation seeking non-network option proposals to
  meet the identified investment needs for three newly actionable projects
  confirmed in the 2026 Integrated System Plan (ISP), published 25 June 2026,
  with submissions due by Thursday 17 September 2026; the 2026 ISP was driven
  substantially by data centre load growth — AEMO's modelling disclosed a 5.4GW
  data centre pipeline nationally, with South Australia flagged as receiving
  increased connection demand from Greater Adelaide, the Mid North and the South
  East as a direct result of data centres and the AI boom — and the ISP's three
  newly actionable projects reflect grid investment needs that are now formally
  open for non-network alternatives including co-located battery storage, demand
  response facilities, and hybrid generation assets; although specific project
  details require review of each consultation notice on the AEMO website, the
  ElectraNet 2026 Transmission Annual Planning Report (TAPR) separately confirms
  that peak electricity demand in South Australia is forecast to double over the
  next 15 years from 3.3GW to more than 6.5GW, driven by data centres, AI load
  and defence industry, and that the estimated AU$3.5 billion Northern
  Transmission Project — spanning the northern and eastern parts of the state
  and connecting Bundey (where IREN's 800MW AI campus is under development) to
  the Adelaide metropolitan load centre and to the anticipated Cultana load
  centre near Port Augusta — is now in active early planning with AER approval
  sought for AU$29.5 million in early-works expenditure; for South Energy, the
  AEMO non-network consultation is directly actionable: sites in the Mid North
  and Spencer Gulf corridor that can offer demand flexibility, co-located storage,
  or hybrid generation have a formal submission pathway to establish grid support
  value before the September 17 close, potentially influencing which network
  investment is required and where grid augmentation dollars flow; the ElectraNet
  TAPR's doubling demand forecast further validates the long-term connection
  scarcity argument for sites that secure early grid agreements.

🏢 3. TECH COMPANY DEMAND - POTENTIAL BUYERS

• Amazon Signs Asia-Pacific's First Standalone BESS Tolling Deal with Anza Power
  for 50MW/200MWh Bairnsdale Battery in Victoria — Confirms Battery-Backed
  Renewable Strategy as the Standard Hyperscale Procurement Template for
  Australian Expansion, Raising the Value of SA Sites Already Adjacent to BESS
  Development Corridors Such as Port Augusta [HIGH RELEVANCE]: Amazon Web
  Services has executed Australia's first standalone battery energy storage
  system (BESS) tolling agreement, signing with Sydney-headquartered Anza Power
  to secure rights to the 50MW/200MWh Bairnsdale Battery Energy Storage System
  in East Gippsland, Victoria — announced 2–3 September 2026 — representing
  Amazon's first standalone battery storage tolling agreement in the Asia-Pacific
  region and the first-of-its-kind arrangement in Australia for any non-energy
  business; under the structure, Amazon gains contractual access to energy stored
  in the Bairnsdale BESS, the right to control charging and discharging cycles,
  and grid arbitrage capability, in exchange for a regular fixed fee paid to Anza
  Power, without carrying construction, ownership or maintenance obligations for
  the storage facility; the deal forms part of Amazon's AU$20 billion (US$13.86
  billion) commitment to expand Australian data centre infrastructure by 2029,
  supplementing nine existing Power Purchase Agreements for new renewable
  generation across the country; the Amazon-Anza structure establishes a new
  procurement archetype that is highly material to South Energy's buyer universe:
  hyperscalers are now purchasing firm renewable capacity and grid flexibility at
  the individual asset level — not via aggregated or legacy renewable energy
  certificates — meaning buyers entering Australia's compliance environment under
  the proposed REGO additionality and firming requirements can satisfy the
  mandatory firming component through standalone BESS tolling agreements without
  building or owning storage; for South Energy, this deal is a direct signal that
  SA sites co-located with, or in close proximity to, committed BESS projects —
  including the AMPYR-GGE 270MW/2,160MWh Northern Battery at Port Augusta
  (which received its AEMO 5.3.4 connection approval in August 2026 and is
  targeting financial close and construction commencement in Q4 2026) — now carry
  a premium buyer-side attribute: a hyperscale-ready firming solution that
  replicates the Amazon-Anza structure and satisfies the AEMC framework's firming
  requirement without requiring the buyer to own or operate battery infrastructure;
  the Port Augusta corridor specifically — where Firmus Technologies holds up to
  1.3GW of planned IT load capacity at Stirling North and the AMPYR Northern
  Battery is approaching construction — is now the closest Australian analogue to
  a fully integrated hyperscale-ready site combining renewable supply, grid
  connection, international data connectivity, and contracted battery firming in
  a single geographic cluster.

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• AEMC Four-Part Data Centre Energy Framework Now Before Energy and Climate
  Change Ministerial Council for September 2026 Formal Consideration — REGO
  Additionality Rule, Firming Mandate, Market Registration and Flexible
  Connection Requirements Enter the Formal Legislative Pipeline, Hardening the
  Compliance Calculus That Advantages SA Projects [HIGH RELEVANCE]: The
  Australian Energy Market Commission (AEMC) has advanced National Electricity
  Rule change requests to the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council
  (ECMC) for formal ministerial consideration in September 2026, following the
  public release of the AEMC's full regulatory advice on 5 August 2026; the
  AEMC's four-part framework requires large data centres to: (1) surrender
  Renewable Electricity Guarantee of Origin (REGO) certificates linked
  exclusively to new renewable energy generation — the additionality requirement
  that closes the compliance loophole allowing purchase of certificates from
  existing wind or solar plants without triggering a single megawatt of new
  capacity; (2) contract alongside REGOs for firming capacity, making battery
  storage the de facto compliance mechanism under the federal framework; (3)
  register as formal market participants under National Electricity Market rules;
  and (4) structure grid connection agreements specifically to enable flexible,
  co-located, demand-response-capable operation; the AEMC estimates 12 months to
  implement the REGO obligation from ministerial endorsement and 24 to 36 months
  for the market registration and connections reforms; the Mallesons analysis
  described the combined ECMC decisions of 5 August 2026 as "a big day for data
  centres," encompassing not only the AEMC energy obligation advice but also new
  cost-recovery rules requiring data centres to pay their full share of new grid
  connection costs, ending the cross-subsidisation model that had effectively
  hidden grid augmentation expenses across the broader consumer base; for South
  Energy, the AEMC framework moving from advice into the formal ECMC legislative
  process is the sharpest tightening of the compliance environment yet seen:
  buyers cannot satisfy the REGO additionality requirement by purchasing legacy
  green certificates, cannot avoid firming obligations with REGOs alone, and
  must now pay full cost-reflective connection charges — all of which make sites
  that already carry contracted new renewable supply, battery firming arrangements
  and approved grid connections categorically more valuable than greenfield sites
  in states where compliance requires building the entire renewable-firming stack
  from scratch; South Australia's existing contracted renewable pipeline,
  combined with the AMPYR Northern Battery approval and the Firmus-SUBCO
  connectivity infrastructure, means the SA corridor is the only region in
  Australia where the full AEMC compliance stack is already being assembled by
  third parties, without requiring the buyer to originate each component.

🔌 1. 電網接入機會

• AEMO就2026年綜合系統規劃三個新增可執行項目啟動非網路方案諮詢——
  截止日期為2026年9月17日，為儲能及需求響應方提交電網支援替代方案
  創造即時窗口【高度相關】：澳大利亞能源市場運營商（AEMO）已就2026年
  《綜合系統規劃》（ISP，於2026年6月25日發布）中確認的三個新增可執行
  項目，正式啟動非網路方案提案諮詢，截止日期為2026年9月17日（星期四）；
  本次2026年ISP很大程度上受數據中心負荷增長驅動——AEMO的建模揭示全國
  數據中心管道容量達5.4吉瓦，南澳州因數據中心和AI熱潮的直接推動，被列
  為來自大阿德萊德地區、中北部及東南部的連接需求顯著增加的地區——三個
  新增可執行項目反映的電網投資需求，目前已正式對外開放非網路替代方案
  申請，包括配套電池儲能、需求響應設施及混合發電資產；此外，ElectraNet
  2026年《輸電年度規劃報告》（TAPR）另行確認，受數據中心、AI負荷及國防
  工業帶動，南澳州的電力需求峰值預計在未來15年內從3.3吉瓦翻倍至逾
  6.5吉瓦；估值約35億澳元的北部輸電項目（Northern Transmission Project）
  ——覆蓋該州北部及東部，連接邦迪（Bundey，IREN 800兆瓦AI園區正在此
  開發）與阿德萊德都會負荷中心及奧古斯塔港附近預期中的Cultana負荷中心
  ——現已進入積極前期規劃階段，AER已收到2,950萬澳元前期施工支出的批准
  申請；對South Energy而言，AEMO非網路方案諮詢具備直接可執行性：中北部
  及斯賓塞灣走廊中能夠提供需求靈活性、配套儲能或混合發電的場址，擁有在
  9月17日截止前提交正式申請的途徑，有潛力影響所需電網投資的規模和流向；
  ElectraNet TAPR中需求倍增的預測，進一步印證了早期取得電網協議的場址
  所具備的長期連接稀缺性論點。【高度相關】

🏢 3. 科技公司需求（潛在買家）

• 亞馬遜與Anza Power簽署亞太地區首個獨立電池儲能系統（BESS）託管協議，
  標的為維多利亞州50兆瓦/200兆瓦時貝恩斯代爾電池——確認電池支撐可再生
  能源策略已成超大規模雲端客戶澳大利亞擴張的標準採購範式，使鄰近BESS
  開發走廊（如奧古斯塔港）的南澳州場址價值大幅提升【高度相關】：亞馬遜
  雲端服務（AWS）於2026年9月2至3日宣布，與總部位於悉尼的Anza Power就
  維多利亞州吉普斯蘭地區50兆瓦/200兆瓦時貝恩斯代爾電池儲能系統（BESS）
  簽署澳大利亞首個獨立BESS託管協議，成為亞馬遜在亞太地區就獨立電池儲能
  系統達成的首份託管協議，也是澳大利亞非能源業企業的首例同類安排；根據
  協議，亞馬遜獲得貝恩斯代爾BESS所儲電能的合約使用權、充放電調度控制
  權及電網套利能力，以向Anza Power支付固定費用作為對價，無需承擔儲能
  設施的建設、所有權或運營責任；此協議是亞馬遜兌現其2029年前在澳大利亞
  擴展數據中心基礎設施200億澳元（約合138.6億美元）承諾的組成部分，
  並對現有9份可再生能源購電協議（PPA）形成補充；亞馬遜與Anza Power的
  合作結構確立了一種對South Energy買方群體極具參考價值的新採購範式：
  超大規模雲端客戶正以個別資產層面的獨立BESS託管方式購入堅實可再生
  能源容量和電網靈活性，而非依賴匯總或歷史可再生能源證書——這意味著
  在擬議中的REGO附加性和調峰要求下進入澳大利亞合規環境的買方，可通過
  獨立BESS託管協議滿足強制調峰義務，而無需建設或持有儲能資產；對
  South Energy而言，與已落實BESS項目毗鄰或緊靠的南澳州場址——包括
  AMPYR-GGE於奧古斯塔港開發的270兆瓦/2,160兆瓦時北部電池（Northern
  Battery，已於2026年8月獲AEMO 5.3.4條款連接批准，目標於2026年第四
  季度完成財務決策並開工建設）——現已具備一項符合超大規模採購標準的
  電網調峰解決方案，可複製亞馬遜-Anza結構，在買方無需擁有或運營電池

📜 4. 政策與法規（南澳州 + 全國）

• AEMC四部分數據中心能源框架規則修訂申請提交能源與氣候變化部長理事會
  （ECMC）進行2026年9月正式審議——REGO附加性規定、強制調峰要求、市場
  主體登記及靈活接入要求正式進入立法管道，進一步強化有利於南澳州項目的
  合規邏輯【高度相關】：澳大利亞能源市場委員會（AEMC）已於2026年9月
  將相關《全國電力規則》修訂申請提交能源與氣候變化部長理事會（ECMC）
  進行正式審議，此前AEMC於2026年8月5日公開發布了其就數據中心能源義務
  監管路徑提供的完整建議；AEMC的四部分框架要求大型數據中心：（一）須
  繳交與全新可再生能源發電項目掛鉤的可再生電力原產地保證書（REGO）——
  附加性要求堵住了從現有風電或光伏項目購入證書而無需拉動任何新增裝機
  容量的合規漏洞；（二）在繳交REGO的同時，須另行合約落實調峰容量，
  使電池儲能成為聯邦框架下的事實合規工具；（三）依據全國電力市場（NEM）
  規則完成正式市場主體登記；（四）電網接入協議須具體設計為支持靈活、
  配套及需求響應能力的運行模式；AEMC估計從部長批准到完成REGO義務的
  實施約需12個月，市場主體登記及接入改革則需24至36個月；Mallesons的
  分析將2026年8月5日ECMC的系列決定描述為「數據中心行業的大日子」，
  涵蓋AEMC能源義務建議及電網接入成本分攤新規則，新規則要求數據中心
  承擔其電網新接入費用的全額成本，終結了此前實際上由更廣大消費者群體
  交叉補貼電網擴容費用的模式；對South Energy而言，AEMC框架從建議意見
  正式進入ECMC立法程序，是迄今為止合規環境收緊程度最高的信號：買方
  無法以歷史綠色證書滿足REGO附加性要求，無法僅憑REGO規避調峰義務，
  且現在須支付完全成本反映的接入費用——上述所有要求，均使已落實新可再
  生能源供應合約、電池調峰安排及批准電網連接的場址，在類別層面明顯
  優於其他州的未開發場址（後者需要買方從零開始構建完整的可再生能源
  調峰合規體系）；南澳州現有已合約化可再生能源管道、AMPYR北部電池
  獲批及Firmus-SUBCO連通基礎設施三者疊加，意味著南澳走廊是澳大利亞
  唯一一個完整AEMC合規技術棧已由第三方在同一地理集群範圍內構建中的
  地區，無需買方逐一自行原創各個組成部分。【高度相關】
