🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] IREN secures 330kV transmission connection at Bundey substation for 800MW data center campus — the agreement provides four 330kV feeder exits supporting up to 800MW without requiring network upgrades, confirming Bundey as a premium grid-connected site in SA.
  Source: GlobeNewswire (June 2026) — https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/03/3305925/0/en/IREN-Announces-First-Australian-Data-Center-Campus-800MW-in-South-Australia.html

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] AEMO discloses 5.4GW Australian data centre pipeline moving through the national transmission connection queue as of June 2026 — SA represents a growing share, driven by Bundey site and expanding grid capacity from EnergyConnect.
  Source: Australia Data Centre Index / CertifiedStrategic (June 2026) — https://certifiedstrategic.com/insights/aemo-5-4gw-australian-data-centre-pipeline-june-2026

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] ElectraNet's 2026 Transmission Annual Planning Report (TAPR) flags increased connection demand in Greater Adelaide, Mid North and South East SA driven by data centres, AI load growth and defence — three new transmission projects proposed: Northern Transmission, Eyre Peninsula Upgrade (Cultana-Yadnarie), and South East Expansion.
  Source: ESD News / ElectraNet TAPR (March 2026) — https://esdnews.com.au/tapr-highlights-vital-transmission-projects-for-sas-growth/

• Project EnergyConnect — Australia's first SA-NSW 330kV interconnector — is approaching full 800MW transfer capability, with full release targeted mid-2026 following Stage 2 commissioning; this unlocks significant new export and import capacity for SA grid users.
  Source: Utility Magazine / Project EnergyConnect — https://utilitymagazine.com.au/project-energyconnect-successfully-delivering-electranets-largest-transmission-project/

• AMPYR Australia's 270MW Northern Battery BESS near Port Augusta received AEMO's 5.3.4 letter, confirming grid compliance; financial close and construction targeted Q4 2026 — adds firming capacity to the Mid North corridor.
  Source: Energy-Storage.News — https://www.energy-storage.news/ampyr-australia-bags-5-3-4-letter-for-270mw-grid-forming-battery-storage-system/

• South Australia's Technical Regulator issued guidelines (February 2026) requiring data centres of 100MW or more to obtain certification covering power system reliability, security and stability, including mandatory backup systems and compliance during grid stress events.
  Source: Ashurst / Australian Electricity and Gas Markets Update — https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/energy-alert-australian-electricity-and-gas-markets-february-2026-update/


☀️ 2. RENEWABLE ENERGY & SITE CONDITIONS

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] South Australia opened 11,000 sq km of Crown land for large-scale renewable energy feasibility licences under the Hydrogen and Renewable Energy Act: Whyalla West (6,500 sq km, Upper Spencer Gulf) and Gawler Ranges East (5,200 sq km, Upper Eyre Peninsula) — applications closed 28 June 2026; successful licensees gain exclusive rights to develop solar, wind, and storage on strategic parcels.
  Source: ESD News / pv-magazine-australia (April 2026) — https://esdnews.com.au/south-australia-opens-new-areas-for-renewables-investment/

• SA's renewable project pipeline stands at A$32.5 billion in large-scale projects either under construction, under assessment, or approved — underpinning South Energy's proposition that co-located land offers renewable-backed power certainty for tech buyers.
  Source: SA Government Energy & Mining — https://www.energymining.sa.gov.au/home/news/latest/south-australia-open-for-more-renewable-energy-investment

• AEMO's 2026 Integrated System Plan (released 25 June 2026) reaffirms renewables + storage + gas as the least-cost path to reliable electricity to 2050 — SA's existing high-renewables profile positions the state favourably for tech company ESG commitments.
  Source: ESD News / AEMO ISP — https://esdnews.com.au/aemo-unveils-2026-integrated-system-plan/

• SA is targeting 100% net renewable electricity by 2027, already holding the record as Australia's first grid to reach that threshold on an average basis — a key buying signal differentiator for hyperscalers with net-zero pledges.
  Source: RenewEconomy — https://reneweconomy.com.au/ai-giant-choose-australias-first-100-pct-net-renewable-grid-to-build-countrys-biggest-data-centre/

• No significant new renewable site announcements in the last 48 hours beyond the above context.

  Source: GlobeNewswire / RenewEconomy (June 2026) — https://reneweconomy.com.au/ai-giant-choose-australias-first-100-pct-net-renewable-grid-to-build-countrys-biggest-data-centre/

• Partners Group acquired Australian data center operator GreenSquareDC, signalling continued institutional capital appetite for Australian data center assets and potential buyer interest in sites with grid and planning certainty.
  Source: Data Center Dynamics — https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/partners-group-acquires-australian-data-center-firm-greensquaredc/

  Source: Yahoo Finance / w.media — https://w.media/australias-data-centre-investment-pipeline-to-reach-usd-15-51-billion/

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] The Australian Government announced on 15 July 2026 that it will legislate mandatory AI standards for large-scale data centres — National Cabinet is expected to consider the framework in August 2026, with legislation targeted for early 2027. Key requirements include data centres underwriting new power supply and putting "at least as much energy into the grid as they take out."
  Source: Gilbert + Tobin / White & Case / HSF Kramer (July 2026) — https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/australian-government-announces-mandatory-ai-standards-for-large-scale-data-centres-and-new-office-of-ai

• [HIGH RELEVANCE] The Australian Government's five National Expectations for data centre developers (released 23 March 2026) set out that developers must: support national security and data sovereignty; fund new and additional clean energy generation or storage; pay their share of grid connection and transmission costs; adopt industry-leading energy efficiency; and support grid stability through demand flexibility. These expectations are now shaping approvals.
  Source: Department of Industry Science and Resources / Norton Rose Fulbright — https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers

• A new centralised Office of AI has been established within the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet to manage national AI policy and fast-track approvals — relevant for South Energy projects requiring federal sign-off.
  Source: Gilbert + Tobin — https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/australian-government-announces-mandatory-ai-standards-for-large-scale-data-centres-and-new-office-of-ai

• SA's reliability gap for December 2026 to February 2027 has been formally closed by AEMO (notified to AER October 2025) — SA's grid stability outlook is now positive for near-term large load connections.
  Source: Australian Energy Regulator — https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/retail/reliability-obligation/south-australia-december-2026-february-2027/t-3

• SA Planning and Design Code Version 2026.1 released January 2026 — the updated code governs industrial zones and data centre permissibility; project proponents should verify zoning under the current version.
  Source: SA DIT — https://dit.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/1580066/South-Australian-Planning-and-Design-Code-Version-2026-1-15-January-2026.pdf
