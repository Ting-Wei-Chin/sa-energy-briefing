🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• ElectraNet's 2026 Transmission Annual Planning Report (TAPR) forecasts rising connection
  demand from data centres and AI across Greater Adelaide, the Mid North, and South East
  regions, and proposes a $3.5 billion Northern Transmission project to expand capacity
  between Bundey substation and the Adelaide metropolitan load centre — directly supporting
  grid access for projects in or near the Bundey corridor. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: ESD News | https://esdnews.com.au/tapr-highlights-vital-transmission-projects-for-sas-growth/
  Source: pv magazine Australia | https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2026/03/31/electranet-unveils-transmission-plans-to-support-demand-growth/

• Project EnergyConnect — the 900km, 800MW SA–NSW interconnector — reached full transfer
  capability in mid-2026, adding major renewable firming capacity to South Australia's grid
  and materially improving large-load connection viability for prospective data centre sites. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: Utility Magazine | https://utilitymagazine.com.au/project-energyconnect-successfully-delivering-electranets-largest-transmission-project/

• AEMO disclosed a 5.4 GW Australian data centre pipeline in June 2026 — well above prior
  forecasts — with significant SA capacity; AEMO also confirmed the previously flagged 25 MW
  reliability gap for SA in 2026–27 is no longer forecast, improving near-term connection
  prospects for new loads.
  Source: Australia Data Centre Index | https://certifiedstrategic.com/insights/aemo-5-4gw-australian-data-centre-pipeline-june-2026
  Source: AER | https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/retail/reliability-obligation/south-australia-december-2026-february-2027/t-3

• AMPYR Australia secured an AEMO 5.3.4 technical clearance letter for a 270 MW grid-forming
  BESS near Port Augusta, targeting financial close and construction in Q4 2026 — this storage
  project will strengthen grid stability in the SA Mid North corridor, enhancing reliability
  for co-located data centre and renewable projects.
  Source: Energy Storage News | https://www.energy-storage.news/ampyr-australia-bags-5-3-4-letter-for-270mw-grid-forming-battery-storage-system/

• South Australia's Technical Regulator (February 2026) now requires data centres of 100 MW
  or more to obtain power system certification covering reliability, security, stability,
  backup energy systems, and compliance with system stress directions — a critical new
  compliance requirement for all large-scale project developers to integrate from day one. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: Ashurst | https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/australian-electricity-and-gas-markets-april-2026-update/


☀️ 2. RENEWABLE ENERGY & SITE CONDITIONS

• South Australia opened more than 11,000 sq km of Crown land for large-scale renewable energy

  Whyalla West (~6,500 sq km, Upper Spencer Gulf); feasibility licence applications under the
  Hydrogen and Renewable Energy Act closed 28 June 2026 — sites in these zones with dual-use
  potential (renewable generation + data centre hosting) represent high-value acquisition
  targets. [HIGH RELEVANCE]

• SA's $32.5 billion pipeline of large-scale renewable energy projects (under construction,
  under assessment, or approved) continues to support the state's 100% net renewables by 2027
  target, signalling abundant co-location development potential for data centre projects seeking
  long-term renewable power purchase agreements.
  Source: SA Government | https://www.energymining.sa.gov.au/industry/hydrogen-and-renewable-energy/leading-the-green-economy

• AEMO's 2026 Integrated System Plan confirms renewables + storage + transmission is

  Source: Energy Storage News | https://www.energy-storage.news/australias-battery-storage-fleet-triples-daily-load-shifting-as-4-4gw-comes-online-in-q1-2026-says-aemo/


🏢 3. TECH COMPANY DEMAND - POTENTIAL BUYERS

  centre campus at Bundey, SA (~125 km northeast of Adelaide), securing four 330kV feeder
  exits at Bundey substation without requiring network upgrades — the largest announced data
  centre project in Australian history, and a clear market signal that SA's renewable grid
  differentiates it from other Australian states for hyperscale AI compute. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: GlobeNewswire | https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/03/3305925/0/en/IREN-Announces-First-Australian-Data-Center-Campus-800MW-in-South-Australia.html

  $630–$725 billion in 2026, approximately A$45 billion (~US$31B) of which has already been
  committed to Australian digital infrastructure in a 22-month window — confirming sustained,
  structural demand for data centre sites across Australia.

  Source: CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/06/google-microsoft-meta-amazon-ai-cash.html

  institutional capital is actively building a national data centre land bank, and that

• AEMO's 5.4 GW national data centre pipeline (June 2026) — far exceeding previous market
  estimates — confirms accelerating demand from hyperscalers and AI operators; SA's 100%
  (net) renewable grid target by 2027 positions it as the preferred jurisdiction for operators
  needing clean power credentials.
  Source: Australia Data Centre Index | https://certifiedstrategic.com/insights/aemo-5-4gw-australian-data-centre-pipeline-june-2026


📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• The Australian Government announced on 15 July 2026 its intention to legislate mandatory AI
  standards for large-scale data centres; National Cabinet is expected to consider the approach
  in August 2026, with legislation anticipated in early 2027. Future data centres will be
  legally required to underwrite new renewable power supply and be net energy contributors to
  the grid — developers must factor these obligations into site acquisition and project
  structuring now. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: GT Law | https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/australian-government-announces-mandatory-ai-standards-for-large-scale-data-centres-and-new-office-of-ai
  Source: White & Case | https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/australian-ai-update-pms-ai-and-data-centre-speech-key-takeaways-data-centre

• The federal government's voluntary Data Centre Expectations (published 23 March 2026)
  require operators to underwrite new renewable power supply, pay full new grid connectivity
  costs, and support demand flexibility — these are expected to transition to mandatory
  requirements under forthcoming 2027 legislation; early compliance builds competitive
  advantage in vendor selection by hyperscalers.
  Source: Department of Industry | https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers
  Source: Norton Rose Fulbright | https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/746b2689/new-expectations-for-data-centre-operators-and-ai-infrastructure-developers

• South Australia implemented the Mandatory Offer Mechanism (MMO) in July 2026, reshaping how
  generators offer into the SA wholesale market — data centre developers with co-located
  generation assets must understand and account for new trading obligations from the outset.
  Source: Ashurst | https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/australian-electricity-and-gas-markets-april-2026-update/

• AEMO's Grid Reference Transitional Service commenced 1 July 2026 to maintain power system
  security during minimum synchronous generator conditions in SA, following expiry of existing
  NSCAS contracts — a technical milestone affecting system security terms for large new loads
  seeking connection in SA.
  Source: AEMO | https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/electricity/national-electricity-market-nem/nem-forecasting-and-planning/transition-planning/transitional-services---type-1-services/south-australian-grid-reference-transitional-service
