🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• AEMO's 2026 Electricity System Outlook (ESOO) discloses that Australia's active data centre
  grid connection pipeline has reached 5.4 GW as at June 2026 — confirming that data centres
  are now the single largest source of new connection requests in the National Electricity
  Market and that demand for grid capacity is growing faster than new transmission can be
  built; for South Energy, this pipeline figure underscores the premium value of sites that
  already hold a confirmed or reserved grid connection pathway, as any data centre buyer
  entering the market today faces a queue measured in gigawatts; sites in the Bundey–Port
  Augusta corridor — where ElectraNet's Northern Transmission Project will add material
  new transfer capacity — sit at the front of this queue. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: Australia Data Centre Index / Certified Strategic |
  https://certifiedstrategic.com/insights/aemo-5-4gw-australian-data-centre-pipeline-june-2026

• The South Australian Grid Reference Transitional Service (a Type 1 transitional service
  procured by AEMO) commenced on 1 July 2026, replacing expiring Network Support and Control
  Ancillary Services (NSCAS) contracts; the service maintains power system security under
  minimum synchronous generator conditions in SA while new grid-forming battery storage
  projects — including AMPYR's 270 MW Northern Battery — come online; this measure removes a
  regulatory uncertainty that could otherwise have delayed new large-load connection approvals
  in SA, smoothing the path for data centre connection applications in the state.
  Source: AEMO |
  https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/electricity/national-electricity-market-nem/
  nem-forecasting-and-planning/transition-planning/transitional-services---type-1-services/
  south-australian-grid-reference-transitional-service

• New rules governing data centre connections to the National Electricity Market — covering
  grid connection obligations, renewable energy underwriting, and network cost recovery for
  large loads — were recently flagged by King & Wood Mallesons as a "big day for data
  centres," reflecting rapid AEMC and federal government rule changes affecting how large
  energy users connect and pay for grid upgrades; the new cost-recovery framework directly
  affects the financial terms on which South Energy can present grid-connected sites to
  prospective data centre buyers, and projects that have already secured connection agreements
  will be insulated from future cost-recovery rule changes applied to late arrivals. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: King & Wood Mallesons |
  https://www.mallesons.com/au/en/insights/latest-thinking/big-day-for-data-centres.html


☀️ 2. RENEWABLE ENERGY & SITE CONDITIONS

• Grid-scale and distributed solar generation broke new quarterly records across Australia in
  Q1 2026, according to AEMO's April 2026 data; South Australia — the only Australian state
  that regularly achieves 100% net renewable penetration — captured disproportionate benefit
  from record solar output, reinforcing the energy cost and ESG credentials of SA data centre
  sites relative to gas-heavy grids in NSW and Victoria; renewable overgeneration conditions
  in SA also create structural downward pressure on long-term PPA pricing, strengthening the
  economic case for renewable-powered data centres — the key financial advantage South Energy
  can offer buyers who must comply with the federal government's new net-positive energy
  mandate (see Policy section).

📜 4. POLICY & REGULATION (SA + NATIONAL)

• FEDERAL MANDATORY FRAMEWORK — HIGH RELEVANCE: The Australian Government announced in
  July 2026 mandatory AI and energy standards for large-scale data centres and established
  a new Office of AI; the framework legally requires large-scale AI data centres to:
  (i) underwrite new renewable power supply; (ii) "put at least as much energy into our grid
  as they take out of it" — a net-positive energy mandate eliminating the option of simply
  connecting to an existing grid; and (iii) make compute available to Australian AI start-ups;
  for South Energy, this federal mandate is commercially transformative — it means no
  hyperscale buyer can proceed in Australia without also acquiring new renewable supply, which
  is precisely the integrated land-plus-renewable model South Energy is structuring; projects
  that arrive with both land and contracted renewable generation will carry a decisive
  regulatory and commercial advantage over buyers still seeking to assemble these components
  separately; SA's existing renewable pipeline and the federal mandate together create a
  structural premium for South Energy's site package. [HIGH RELEVANCE]
  Source: GT Law |

• FEDERAL EXPECTATIONS FRAMEWORK (March 2026): The Australian Government published its
  National Expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure developers — five expectations
  covering (i) national security and data sovereignty, (ii) energy underwriting obligations,
  (iii) full cost-sharing for new grid connectivity, (iv) demand flexibility participation,
  and (v) access for Australian AI start-ups; while not creating immediate legal obligations
  (the July mandatory framework addresses that), projects aligned with these expectations
  receive prioritised treatment through Commonwealth regulatory processes including Foreign
  Investment Review Board (FIRB) approvals and planning coordination; South Energy's SA
  projects, structured to align with both the federal expectations and SA's own Data Centre
  and AI Infrastructure Act, should be positioned to prospective buyers as "regulator-ready"
  — a material risk and timeline advantage at the deal-structuring stage. [HIGH RELEVANCE]

  資源。對South Energy而言，這一聯邦強制要求在商業上具有決定性意義——任何超大規模買家在澳大利亞

  雖然此框架目前尚未直接形成法律義務（2026年7月強制性框架已完成這一步），但符合期望標準的項目將
  在聯邦監管流程中獲得優先對待，包括澳大利亞外商投資審核委員會（FIRB）審批及規劃協調等；South
  Energy按聯邦和南澳州雙重標準開發的項目，應向潛在買家定位為「監管就緒（Regulator-Ready）」型
  資產，在項目交易結構階段具有實質性的風險和時間節點優勢。【高度相關】
