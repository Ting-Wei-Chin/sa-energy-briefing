🔌 1. GRID & CONNECTION OPPORTUNITIES

• MAJOR NEW PLANNING DOCUMENT — ElectraNet 2026 TAPR & Northern Transmission Project
  ($3.5 Billion): ElectraNet's just-published 2026 Transmission Annual Planning Report
  (TAPR) formally flags three major proposed transmission projects in South Australia:
  (i) the Northern Transmission Project (NTx, est. $3.5 billion) in early planning stages,
  which would add large new transfer capacity between Bundey and the Adelaide metropolitan
  load centre, and between Bundey and the proposed Cultana East load centre — directly
  opening new large-load connection pathways from the Bundey corridor where South Energy's
  sites are concentrated; (ii) the Eyre Peninsula Upgrade (est. $350 million), which would
  upgrade the Cultana–Yadnarie lines from 132 kV to 275 kV by 2027/28 and establish a new
  275/132 kV Yadnarie North substation; and (iii) a South East Expansion project. AEMO
  concurrently released the Northern Transmission Project Assessment Draft Report (April
  2026), the official project assessment document that will govern approvals. South
  Australia's peak electricity demand is independently forecast to double from 3.3 GW today
  to over 6.5 GW within 15 years, driven by data centres, green steel, and hydrogen — the
  TAPR projects are a direct response to this demand trajectory. For South Energy, the NTx
  project is the single most important infrastructure development in the SA grid pipeline:
  sites in the Bundey corridor are positioned to benefit from the upgraded capacity and will
  face a shorter connection queue than any site connected after this infrastructure is
  committed. [HIGH RELEVANCE]

• Project EnergyConnect — Full 800 MW Transfer Capability Approaching: The 900 km
  NSW–SA interconnector (Project EnergyConnect), the largest transmission project ever
  delivered by ElectraNet and Australia's first direct link between the South Australian
  and New South Wales grids, is nearing the release of its full 800 MW transfer capacity;
  the SA component includes South Australia's first-ever 330/275 kV substation at Bundey;
  full 800 MW capability release is targeted for mid-2026 following Stage 2 internetwork
  testing with Transgrid's NSW component; the completed interconnector will materially
  expand South Australia's ability to import and export power reliably, reducing data centre
  energy cost risk and strengthening the network security case for large new SA loads.

☀️ 2. RENEWABLE ENERGY & SITE CONDITIONS

• South Australia Opens 11,000+ sq km for Large-Scale Renewable Energy Investment — Direct
  Co-Location Opportunity: The South Australian Government has released two new renewable
  energy development areas comprising over 11,000 square kilometres: the Gawler Ranges
  East release area (approximately 5,200 sq km on the Upper Eyre Peninsula) and the Whyalla
  West release area (approximately 6,500 sq km in the Upper Spencer Gulf region, adjacent to
  the Bundey–Port Augusta corridor); the two areas include some of the highest co-incident
  wind and solar resources in South Australia and are estimated to have the potential to
  power more than 500,000 homes; the government invited international investors to propose
  large-scale solar, wind, and storage projects across these areas under the state's
  renewable energy framework, with a tender window that closed 28 June 2026; the Whyalla
  West release area in particular overlaps with the geographic corridor in which South
  Energy is acquiring land, creating the potential to co-locate a renewable energy project
  directly alongside a data centre campus — the integrated land-plus-renewable model that
  the federal mandatory framework now requires of all hyperscale data centre operators in
  Australia. [HIGH RELEVANCE]

  而言，北部輸電項目是南澳州電網發展管線中最具戰略意義的基礎設施進展：班迪走廊內的地塊將率先
